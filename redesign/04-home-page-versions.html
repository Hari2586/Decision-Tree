Three versions of the MoneyHoney home page

Built from current finance website patterns, with every word, link and number of the page kept exactly as it is. Same brand navy and orange, same Ledger buttons and small radii. Pick one, or name the parts you want combined.

What the research says

The Dribbble search page itself cannot be read by a script, so the shot-level evidence comes from Dribbble's tag pages as indexed by search, and the pattern evidence from 2026 trend and teardown articles. Studio or agency sources are noted as such.

1

Dark navy with a warm glow is the dominant premium look. The most-liked finance shots (Gabrun by Plainthing, Cashbank by Dipa Inhouse, the Keitoto wealth pages) are tagged dark, glow, clean and blue; 2026 roundups call dark mode close to a requirement for data-heavy fintech and name Robinhood, Coinbase and Binance.Shaped Version 1.

2

Bento grids moved from Apple keynotes to fintech feature sections. Trend guides list asymmetric tile grids and oversized typography among the year's top landing patterns, with tiles that respond on hover and collapse to one column on phones.Shaped Version 2.

3

Heroes show a mechanism, not a slogan. Teardowns group fintech heroes into product preview, calculator, flow visual and data visual, and favour real figures over claims. Wise shows literal fees; Wealthfront leads with data-driven graphs.Shaped Version 3's statement card and bars.

4

Trust sits next to the action. Regulator and licence language near the primary call to action, one primary CTA above the fold, and a security page linked from several places. One guide cites a 10 to 20% lift from compliance badges near CTAs; this is the source's own claim.All three keep the ARN sentence in the lede and the compliance footer; Version 1 and 3 put the regulatory sentence beside the facts.

5

Motion restraint. One ambient animation and one user-triggered interaction per page, with reduced-motion fallbacks. Stripe's slow gradient is the reference.All three keep the existing reveal, count-up and hover set, nothing more.

6

Neutral sans type at 32 to 48px for headlines, 16 to 20px body, 4.5:1 contrast minimum. Suggested faces include Inter, Plus Jakarta Sans and DM Sans; our Manrope and Figtree pairing sits in that family.Unchanged across versions.

The three versions

Version 1

Night Desk

  • Dark navy header and hero with an orange glow, facts as glass tiles with a soft blur.
  • "Find a Solution" stays on the dark band with outlined chips; products return to light cards with navy icon tiles.
  • Footer on dark navy so the page opens and closes in the same colour.

Closest to the Dribbble premium look. Best if you want the site to feel like a modern money app. Costs some of the calm of the current light page.

Open Version 1
Version 2

Bento Board

  • Headline, four facts and the solutions all live in one asymmetric tile grid. One fact tile is navy, one honey.
  • "Start Here" is a bento: Mutual Funds is a large navy tile, Explore Funds a wide tinted one, the rest standard.
  • White page, soft shadows, more air than the current home.

Most on-trend layout and the most scannable on a phone, where tiles stack. Best if you want visitors to feel organised choice.

Open Version 2
Version 3

Split Editorial

  • Two-column hero: the headline left, a navy statement card right with the four facts and a drawn growth line.
  • "Start Here" is a swipeable strip of cards; asset classes become proportional bars sized to their scheme counts; themes a two-column index.
  • Light, quiet, data-led, the least "template" of the three.

Best for first-time investors who need to compare at a glance. The bars turn five lines of numbers into a picture without adding a word.
