Emergency Fund: How Much to Keep and Where

Could you pay your bills for 6 months if your income stopped?

60.7%

Of Indian adults, 60.7% could keep paying their expenses, by any means, for a month or less if their main income stopped.

Source: World Bank Global Findex 2025, India, 2024 survey. Worked out by us: 28.4% (less than 2 weeks) + 32.3% (about a month).[1]

An emergency fund is your own money, kept where you can reach it within days, for the month income stops or a hospital bill arrives. Without one, the gap is filled by family, loans or selling things.

How Long Indian Households Could Last Without Income

  1. 6 in 10 Could Last a Month or Less

    Asked how long they could cover their expenses, by any means, if their main income stopped, 28.4% of Indian adults said less than 2 weeks and 32.3% about a month. Only 22.0% said more than 2 months.[1]

  2. Few Would Use Their Own Savings

    Asked where money for an emergency would mainly come from, 35.3% of Indian adults named family or friends and 13.5% a loan. Only 12.4% named their own savings.[1]

  3. One Hospital Stay Can Be a Large, Sudden Bill

    In 2025, a hospital stay other than for childbirth cost households ₹34,064 out of their own pocket on average, and ₹50,508 in a private hospital.[2]

  4. Most Workers Have No Regular Salary

    In 2025, 56.2% of workers were self-employed and 20.2% casual labourers; 23.6% had a regular wage or salary. Even among salaried employees outside farming, 47.3% were not eligible for paid leave.[3]

Most households would lean on others in an emergency, yet few save for one first. Only 9% of households name building an emergency fund as their main goal for investing.[6]

Where to Keep Your Emergency Fund

  1. Know your number. At the calculator's numbers, 6 months of ₹40,000 comes to ₹2,64,600 by the time the fund is built, as prices rise. After your savings of ₹40,000, the gap is ₹2,18,804.
  2. Fill it month by month. Setting aside ₹8,471 a month for 24 months fills it at the rate you chose: an illustration, not a promise of any return.
  3. Count only what you could not stop paying. Rent, food, bills, EMIs, insurance premiums and school fees make up the monthly figure; spending you could cut in a crisis does not.
  4. Keep a few days' expenses in your bank. A savings account can be reached at any hour, by card or UPI.
  5. Keep the rest where it pays out within days. Liquid and overnight funds hold short-term debt, and some pay part of a redemption the same day. The categories below show how fast.
  6. Keep it out of equity. Money you may need at a week's notice does not belong in equity funds, whose value can fall sharply at any time.
  7. Keep health cover beside it. One stay in a private hospital cost households ₹50,508 out of pocket on average in 2025.[2] Health insurance is meant for bills larger than an emergency fund can carry.
  8. Top it up after you use it. Rebuild the fund once the emergency has passed, and raise it as your expenses rise.

Liquid and Overnight Funds: How Fast Money Comes Out

Where you keep the fund decides how fast the money comes back. These categories hold short-term debt: their value can still fall, and they are not bank deposits.

For the first days

Same Day: Instant Redemption

Some liquid and overnight schemes let resident individuals redeeming online take out up to ₹50,000 a day per scheme, or 90% of the holding's value if that is less, credited the same day. This is SEBI's Instant Access Facility; fund houses may offer it but need not.[7] The rule-based Instant Redemption Funds screen lists the schemes whose documents say they offer it.

For the rest of the fund

Within 3 Working Days: Liquid and Overnight Funds

Overnight funds hold securities that mature in 1 day, and liquid funds debt and money market securities that mature within 91 days.[7] A liquid fund charges an exit load on units redeemed within 7 days of investing, on a falling scale set by AMFI; from the 7th day there is none.[7] The Parking Surplus Cash screen also lists Money Market Funds. Money market funds hold money market instruments that mature within 1 year.[7]

Emergency Fund Questions People Ask

How many months of expenses should an emergency fund cover?

No Indian regulator sets a number. Six months of essential expenses is a common choice; people whose income is irregular, or who support others, often keep more. At the calculator's numbers, 6 months of ₹40,000 comes to ₹2,64,600 once built.

Is a 12-month emergency fund too much?

Not if your income is irregular, you are the only earner, or your work could stop for months. A larger fund has a cost too: money in liquid or overnight funds may grow more slowly over long periods than money invested in equity. Twelve months of the example's ₹40,000 is ₹4,80,000 in today's money.

Can I keep my emergency fund in a liquid fund?

Yes, and many people keep most of it there, with a few days' expenses in the bank. Overnight funds hold securities that mature in 1 day, and liquid funds debt and money market securities that mature within 91 days.[7] A liquid fund is not a bank deposit: its value can fall, and it is not insured by DICGC.

How quickly can I get money out of a liquid fund?

Some liquid and overnight schemes let resident individuals redeeming online take out up to ₹50,000 a day per scheme, or 90% of the holding's value if that is less, credited the same day. This is SEBI's Instant Access Facility; fund houses may offer it but need not.[7] Other redemptions must reach your bank account within 3 working days under SEBI's rules, and often arrive sooner.[7]

What are the risks of a liquid fund?

Liquid funds hold short-term debt, so their value can fall if a borrower defaults or its rating is cut, and it moves a little with interest rates. They are not bank deposits and are not insured by DICGC. Each scheme's riskometer and documents set out its risks.

Is there an exit load on liquid funds?

A liquid fund charges an exit load on units redeemed within 7 days of investing, on a falling scale set by AMFI; from the 7th day there is none.[7] Each scheme's documents give its own load.

How are liquid fund gains taxed?

Liquid, overnight and money market funds are specified mutual funds under the Income-tax Act, 2025, so gains on units bought from 1 Apr 2023 are taxed at your slab rate, however long you hold them.[8] Based on income-tax rules in force on 29 Sep 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Please consult a tax professional.

Should I build an emergency fund before paying off debt?

Many people first set aside a small fund, so that a lost month of income does not become a missed EMI, then clear costly loans such as credit cards and personal loans, then build the rest. The EMI Management page works out what prepaying a loan saves.
