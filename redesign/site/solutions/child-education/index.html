What Drives the Cost of a Child's Education

  1. School Costs Depend on the School

    In 2025, families spent ₹2,863 a year per child at a government school and ₹25,002 at a non-government school, for the academic year then running.[1]

  2. Coaching Adds More at Each Level

    27.0% of school students took private coaching in 2025, and 30.7% in urban areas. Spending on it rose with each level, from ₹525 a year per student at pre-primary to ₹6,384 at higher secondary, and ₹9,950 for higher secondary students in urban areas.[1]

  3. Families Pay Almost All of It

    For 95.0% of school students with education costs, the first source of the money was their own household. Government scholarships came first for 1.2%.[1]

  4. The Jump at College

    In 2017-18, a year of a technical or professional degree cost ₹63,495 on average, more than four times a general degree at ₹14,264. At a private unaided college, engineering cost ₹69,155 a year and medicine ₹94,658.[2]

  5. Education Prices Rise Every Year

    Education prices in the CPI rose every year from 2015 to 2025, by 4.61% a year on average. What cost ₹100 in 2015 cost about ₹157 in 2025.[3]

Costs rise at every stage, and families meet them from their own money. It is the goal most households invest for. 20% of households name their children's education as their main goal for investing, more than any other goal; 4% name retirement.[4]

How Much to Save Each Month for Your Child's Education

  1. Price the course for the year it starts. At the calculator's numbers, a course costing ₹4,00,000 today costs ₹8,31,571 in 15 years, with fees rising 5% a year.
  2. Start the SIP that fills the gap. After what your savings could grow to, the gap is ₹8,31,571: a SIP of ₹1,648 a month at the rate you chose. It is an illustration, not a promise of any return.
  3. Start early: waiting costs. Starting 5 years later, with 10 years to go, needs ₹2,804 a month for the same course, about 1.7 times as much.
  4. Allow for fees rising faster. Education prices in the CPI rose 4.61% a year from 2015 to 2025, and tuition and other fees 4.91%.[3] The example assumes 5%. A sought-after private or overseas course may cost more and rise faster: try the High-Cost Course example above.
  5. Raise the SIP as your income rises. The Step-Up SIP Calculator shows what raising it every year does.
  6. Lower the risk as college nears. In the last few years before the fees fall due, move part of the money from equity towards hybrid and debt funds, so a market fall just before you need it does less harm.
  7. Allow for several years of fees. A degree is paid for over 3 to 5 years, not on one day. A systematic withdrawal plan (SWP) can pay each year's fees while the rest stays invested.

Child Education Questions People Ask

How much should I save each month for my child's education?

It depends on the course, the years left and the rate you assume. In the worked example, a 4-year professional degree costing ₹4,00,000 today needs ₹1,648 a month for 15 years. The High-Cost Course example (₹15,00,000 today, fees rising 8% a year) needs ₹9,430. Enter your child's course above.

How much will education cost in 15 or 20 years?

With fees rising 5% a year, a course costing ₹4,00,000 today costs ₹8,31,571 in 15 years and ₹10,61,319 in 20. Education prices in the CPI rose 4.61% a year from 2015 to 2025, and tuition and other fees 4.91%.[3] Some courses rise faster: change the rate above to see the effect.

Can I invest in a mutual fund in my child's name?

Yes. The folio is in your child's name alone, with no joint holder, and a parent or a court-appointed guardian runs it until your child turns 18. The fund house asks for proof of your child's date of birth and of your relationship.

Can I pay into my child's folio from my own bank account?

Yes. You may pay into your child's folio from your own bank account, your child's, or a joint account of the two. Money taken out goes only to your child's own verified bank account.[5]

What happens to my child's SIP at 18?

When your child turns 18, they complete their own KYC and give their bank details. Until the folio is changed to their name as an adult, the fund house pauses SIPs and accepts no new transactions.[5]

How is a minor's mutual fund income taxed?

While your child is a minor, gains and IDCW from investments in their name are added to the income of the parent who earns more, unless the child earned the money by their own work or skill. In the old tax regime, ₹1,500 a year per child is left out.[7] Based on income-tax rules in force on 29 Sep 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Please consult a tax professional.

What happened to children's funds after SEBI's 2026 changes?

SEBI's scheme categories of 26 Feb 2026 no longer include Children's Funds; Life Cycle Funds, which aim at a target year, take their place. A fund house may continue a Children's Fund it already runs.[6] A continuing fund's lock-in is set in its scheme documents. The time-to-goal table above works with or without one.

Is a mutual fund SIP the same as a child plan from an insurer?

No. An insurer's child plan is an insurance policy, regulated by IRDAI. We do not offer insurance policies, and this page is about investing for a child through mutual funds. NPS Vatsalya, a pension account for a minor under PFRDA, is not a product we offer either; PFRDA's FAQ on it is listed under Read and Share.
