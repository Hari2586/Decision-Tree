Index-style rows instead of card grids. Products compare in aligned columns: name, what it is, go. Navy header and navy compliance footer bracket every page. Orange appears once, on the primary action.

Why it suits a financial-trust audience

  • Looks like a regulated institution, not a startup. Serif headings, hairline rules and tabular figures are the visual language of annual reports and bank statements. First-time investors read that as "established and accountable".
  • Low ink, low anxiety. A white page with navy type and almost no colour fill keeps the eye on words and numbers. Nothing flashes or sells.
  • Compliance is part of the design, not an afterthought. The ARN sits in the hero eyebrow and the footer is a solid navy band, so the regulatory text is the most visible block on the page rather than grey small print.
  • Scales to dense data. Your sub-pages hold 1,774 schemes and screens; row-and-column layouts handle long lists better than cards.

What we avoid

  • Gradients, glassmorphism, big illustrations, stock photos of happy families.
  • Orange on more than one element per screen.
  • Cream or beige backgrounds (the serif-plus-cream look reads as a template).

Goal chips up top so a first-timer picks a problem, not a product. Products sit in equal cards with a two-letter mark, so MF, FD and Bonds compare at a glance. Soft navy bands separate sections. Risk warnings get a boxed callout with an orange edge.

Why it suits a financial-trust audience

  • Familiar to phone-first India. Rounded cards, pill chips and a thumb-sized primary button match the UPI and investing apps your visitors already use, so the site feels safe to tap.
  • Goal-first wayfinding lowers the barrier. "SIP", "Retirement", "Emergency Fund" chips meet first-time investors where they are, before they have to learn what a fund category is.
  • Warmth without hype. Orange is used as a small, consistent mark (icon tiles, one CTA, a callout edge), which signals a human brand while navy keeps the authority.
  • Compliance stays legible. Warnings sit in a bordered callout at body size, not grey footnotes, so they read as care rather than legal cover.

What we avoid

  • Trading-app energy: green and red tickers, charts in the hero, countdown urgency.
  • Purple or blue gradients and floating 3D coins.
  • Emoji as icons; every mark is a typographic tile in brand colours.

What both directions share

  • Content lock. Every headline, label, number, link and disclaimer stays word for word. Sections may be re-ordered on the page; nothing inside a section is re-ordered.
  • Logo untouched. The existing SVG (navy #16205B, orange #FF4A00) is reused as is. The UI palette uses your brief's #1B2666 and #E8511A; the two oranges sit side by side without clashing.
  • Compliance footer on every page, at body size, with the ARN line, the mutual fund risk warning and the SIF risk warning.
  • Mobile-first. 16px gutters, 44px tap targets, one-column stacks under 600px, no horizontal scroll.
  • WCAG AA. All text colours above are 4.5:1 on their backgrounds; orange is used on white only at 18px+ bold or as a fill behind white text.

Reference points

  • Zerodha and Varsity: text-first, ledger-like clarity for Indian investors (feeds A).
  • Vanguard: institutional calm, serif headings, restraint (feeds A).
  • Groww and Kuvera: goal chips, rounded cards, first-timer onboarding (feeds B).
  • Wise: bold type on white, one accent, plain language (feeds B).
  • Your existing design-system notes: "navy does the work", orange "at most one moment per screen", page always white (both).
