Why EMIs Pile Up: Borrowing to Spend

  1. Loans Are Growing Fast

    Bank personal loans reached ₹72,87,218 Crores in August 2026, up 16.9% in a year. Vehicle loans grew fastest.[2]

  2. Half of Borrowing Is for Consumption

    Of what Indian households owe, 49.7% was borrowed for consumption, 33.5% to create assets and 16.8% for productive use.[1]

  3. Consumer Loans Grew Fastest Since 2019

    Since March 2019, households' non-housing retail loans have grown 3.8 times; home loans 2.4 times.[1]

  4. A Long Loan Costs More Than It Looks

    At RBI's average rates on new loans, the interest on a 20-year home loan adds up to 95% of the amount borrowed. On a 5-year personal loan it is 29%.[3]

For most households, EMIs now compete with saving. Across households, loan repayments take about 9% of monthly spending, the same share as investments.[4]

Prepay Your Loan or Invest? Working It Out

  1. Know your share. At the calculator's numbers, EMIs take 33.8% of your take-home income. Many lenders use 40% as a rule of thumb when deciding how much to lend. It is not an RBI rule, and every rupee of EMI is a rupee not invested.
  2. See both paths for the same outgo. Prepaying ₹5,000 a month saves ₹11,76,974 of interest and closes the loan 4 Years 4 Months earlier; investing the whole amount after that illustrates ₹31,30,620 by the loan's original end. Investing the ₹5,000 alongside instead illustrates ₹49,95,740.
  3. Weigh a certain saving against an uncertain return. A prepayment saves the loan's rate for certain. An investment's return is not known in advance: it can be higher or lower than the loan's rate, and it can be negative. The calculator shows both, with no verdict.
  4. Short, costly loans first. RBI's average rates on new loans in August 2026: home loans 7.61%, vehicle loans 9.57%, other personal loans 10.41%.[3] Personal and car loans also run only a few years, which leaves an investment little time to make up for the rate. Many people clear these first: build the prepayment in a liquid fund, then pay it to the lender in one go.
  5. Beside a long home loan, a SIP is the other path. Over 15 to 30 years, an equity SIP has time to ride out market falls. Home-loan interest (up to ₹2,00,000 a year on a home you live in) and principal (within the ₹1,50,000 limit of section 123) reduce tax only in the old tax regime.[7] The calculator leaves tax out.
  6. Check the charges before you prepay. Since 1 Jan 2026, banks and NBFCs cannot charge an individual for paying off a floating-rate loan taken for purposes other than business early, in part or in full. This applies to existing floating-rate term loans too.[5][6] A fixed-rate loan may carry a pre-payment charge, but only if the lender disclosed it in the sanction letter and the Key Facts Statement, and only on the amount prepaid.[5][6]
  7. Keep an emergency fund first. A few months of expenses set aside means a lost month of income does not become a missed EMI.

EMI and Loan Questions People Ask

Should I prepay my home loan or invest in a SIP?

The calculator shows both paths for the same monthly outgo. At its numbers, prepaying and then investing illustrates ₹31,30,620 by the loan's end, and investing alongside ₹49,95,740. Prepaying saves the loan's interest for certain; investing may end higher or lower, and its returns are not guaranteed. Tax, an emergency fund and how you feel about owing money matter too.

What is the 40% EMI rule?

A rule of thumb some lenders use when deciding how much to lend: all EMIs together up to about 40% of take-home income. It is not an RBI rule. At the calculator's numbers, your EMIs take 33.8%.

Can a bank charge me for prepaying a loan?

Since 1 Jan 2026, banks and NBFCs cannot charge an individual for paying off a floating-rate loan taken for purposes other than business early, in part or in full. This applies to existing floating-rate term loans too.[5][6] A fixed-rate loan may carry a pre-payment charge, but only if the lender disclosed it in the sanction letter and the Key Facts Statement, and only on the amount prepaid.[5][6] Your loan agreement and Key Facts Statement say which applies.

What if I pay 2 extra EMIs every year?

That is about one-sixth of an EMI more each month. In the worked example, ₹6,769 extra a month on the ₹40,617 EMI closes the loan 5 years 5 months earlier and saves ₹14,56,868 of interest. Enter your own extra amount above to see the effect.

How can I reduce the EMI on an existing personal loan?

Prepaying part of it lowers what you owe, so the EMI or the term can come down; ask the lender which it will change. A longer term lowers the EMI but raises the total interest. Keep enough aside for emergencies first, and check for a charge on a fixed-rate loan.

Does home-loan interest reduce tax in the new regime?

No. Home-loan interest (up to ₹2,00,000 a year on a home you live in) and principal (within the ₹1,50,000 limit of section 123) reduce tax only in the old tax regime.[7] The Old vs New Tax Regime Calculator shows both regimes for your income.
